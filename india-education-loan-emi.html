How an education loan EMI works

An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both interest and part of the loan. Education loans usually give you a moratorium: a break from EMIs while you study, plus a few months after the course.

Interest usually still builds up during the moratorium. If you don't pay it, it is added to your loan, so your EMIs are calculated on a bigger amount.

Example: ₹10,00,000 at 9.5%. Paying nothing during a 5-year moratorium adds about ₹4,75,000 in interest before EMIs even begin.

Banks differ

Some banks charge simple interest during the moratorium, others charge differently. This calculator assumes simple interest during study, then normal EMIs. Ask your bank for its exact method, and about processing fees, which are not included here.

Interest on education loans may qualify for a tax deduction. The rules change, so check the current ones or ask a tax professional.

Common questions

Should I pay interest during my course?

Paying it keeps your loan from growing, so your EMIs and total interest are lower later. But it needs money each month while you're studying. Try both options above to compare.

What is the moratorium?

It is the period with no EMIs. It's usually your course length plus some months, often 6 to 12, but your bank sets it.
