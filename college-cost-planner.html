How this works

We add your four yearly costs, raise them a little each year for price rises, and add up every year of your degree. Then we take away the help you expect to receive, and what's left is what you'd need to cover, often with loans.

Tip: the price rise is a guess. Colleges often raise prices a little each year, so 3% is a cautious starting point.

Cut the gap

Compare in-state and out-of-state costs, ask about scholarships every year, and consider living costs, which are often as big as tuition.

Common questions

What counts as "other" costs?

Bus or car costs, phone, clothes, entertainment and trips home. Students often underestimate these, so be generous.

Are grants and scholarships paid back?

No, grants and scholarships don't need to be repaid. Loans do. The monthly figure shown assumes a 6% loan over 10 years, which is a simple example, not an offer.
