How GPA is calculated

Each grade is worth grade points. You multiply those points by the course's credits, add everything up, then divide by your total credits.

Example: an A (4.0) in a 3-credit course is 12 points. A B (3.0) in a 4-credit course is 12 points. Together that's 24 points over 7 credits, so your GPA is 24 ÷ 7 = 3.43.

Harder or longer courses count for more because they have more credits. That's why one bad grade in a 4-credit class hurts more than in a 1-credit class.

Common questions

Is an A+ worth 4.0 or 4.3?

It depends on your school. Many schools cap at 4.0, and some give an A+ 4.3. This calculator uses 4.0. Check your school's grading policy for the exact scale.

Does this work for weighted GPA?

No. This is an unweighted 4.0 calculator. Weighted GPAs add extra points for honors or AP classes, and schools do that differently.

How do I find my cumulative GPA?

Enter this semester's courses, then fill in your current GPA and the credits you've already finished. The calculator combines them for you.
