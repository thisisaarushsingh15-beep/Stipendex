How this works

First we take your monthly pay before tax and the percentage you'd spend on loans. That gives a monthly payment. Then we work out how large a loan that payment can cover over your repayment years at your interest rate.

Example: $55,000 a year is about $4,583 a month. At 10%, that's about $458 a month towards loans.

Two rules of thumb

People often use two guides: keep your loan payment around 10% of your pay, and don't borrow more in total than your first-year salary. They're rough guides, not rules, and your own costs matter most.

Common questions

Where do I find my expected salary?

Look up typical starting pay for your major and job on your school's career pages or the US Bureau of Labor Statistics. Use a cautious number, not the best case.

Is 10% right for me?

It depends on your rent, other debts and family needs. If your other costs are high, use a lower percentage to stay safe.
