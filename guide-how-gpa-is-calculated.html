How GPA is calculated

A plain-English guide · 5 minute read

Your GPA is an average of your grades, but not a plain one. Courses with more credits count for more. Here's the full picture, step by step.

The formula

GPA = total grade points ÷ total credits, where grade points = the grade's value × the course's credits.

A worked example

CourseGradePointsCreditsGrade points
ChemistryB+3.3413.2
StatisticsA4.0312.0
ArtA-3.727.4
WritingB3.039.0
Total1241.6

41.6 ÷ 12 = 3.47. That's the semester GPA.

Semester GPA vs cumulative GPA

Semester GPA covers one term. Cumulative GPA covers everything so far. To combine them, multiply your old GPA by your old credits, add the new grade points, and divide by all the credits.

Example: you have a 3.20 GPA over 30 credits, then earn the 41.6 points above over 12 credits. (3.20 × 30 + 41.6) ÷ 42 = 3.28.

Why GPA moves slowly

The more credits you've finished, the less one term changes your GPA. With a 3.0 over 60 credits, a perfect 4.0 in a 15-credit semester only lifts you to (180 + 60) ÷ 75 = 3.2. Early terms matter most.

Weighted vs unweighted

An unweighted GPA tops out at 4.0. A weighted GPA gives extra points for honors or AP classes, so it can go above 4.0. Schools do this differently, so use your school's own policy.

Common mistakes

  • Averaging grades without using credits
  • Using the wrong scale, like counting an A+ as 4.3 when your school uses 4.0
  • Counting pass/fail classes, which many schools leave out
  • Forgetting your school's rule for retaken courses, which varies

Outside the US the system can be different. The UK uses degree classes and India often uses CGPA, so this guide covers the US 4.0 scale.
